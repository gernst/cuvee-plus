\section{Principles}\label{sec:principles}

This section collects what the examples have in common: the shape that a proof by lookup takes, how to write a lemma
that the engine applies, how to keep such lemmas sound and the rewriting terminating, and how to find out why a
lemma does not apply.

\subsection{The shape of a proof}\label{sec:principles:shape}
Every proof of this document is built from lemmas of the following kinds, from generic to specific. The first three
are the database's; the others are knowledge about one algorithm, stated at the beginning of its file.
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item \textbf{The database resolves single loops.} Counters, sums, stores and their reads, selects, counts, minima
  and maxima are introduced and accelerated without anything written for the program (Sections~\ref{sec:lsearch},
  \ref{sec:nested} and~\ref{sec:brs2}).
\item \textbf{Inner loops reuse the lemmas of single loops.} An inner loop is a value of the outer update, so the same
  lemmas apply to it where it occurs, with the facts of the outer iteration in the context. Only the numbers of inner
  iterations are fresh; once the constraints determine them, they are a constant list, and the inner traces have a
  known number of steps (Section~\ref{sec:nested}).
\item \textbf{Constraints become named facts.} An iteration constraint that a later step needs at a position is
  replaced by an atom that names it, $\neg\mathsf{occurs}(x, \mathsf{selects}(a, \ldots))$, and a second lemma uses the atom
  at any position (Section~\ref{sec:lsearch}).
\item \textbf{A loop that is only meaningful as a whole gets a name.} A definition of the whole loop with a total
  update is folded where its body occurs, and forward facts about the named loop give the bounds that the
  postcondition needs (Section~\ref{sec:bisect}).
\item \textbf{A summary states what a loop nest computes}, as a rule whose left-hand side is the term of the goal: the
  nest of the transpose (Section~\ref{sec:transpose}), and for the sorts the inner loop as an abstract step, a plain rule
  on the inner loop's term, e.g.\ $\argmin$ (Section~\ref{sec:sorts}).
\item \textbf{An induction lemma states the invariant of an outer loop} as a quantified side condition over an
  abstract step, decided by step lemmas on atoms. No invariant is inferred; its shape is the lemma's knowledge, and
  the engine proves the step.
\item \textbf{Rules on the atoms of the postcondition} characterize a named result: the comparisons with the partition
  points of a sorted array, the regions of a partition, the cases of an exit. The postcondition is never rewritten as
  a whole; each atom finds its rule.
\item \textbf{Name the normal form.} Postconditions are stated in the vocabulary of the database where the
  standard form has no witness or no matchable shape ($\mathsf{occurs}$ rather than $\exists$, $\mathsf{count}$ over
  $\mathsf{selects}$ rather than a quantified multiset equation), and quantified preconditions as atoms of the
  specification. A lemma for a new shape of data lands in the normal form of the old one, so that the rest of the
  proof is shared (Section~\ref{sec:brs2}).
\end{enumerate}
A data-dependent number of iterations is no obstacle to this shape, as long as the property can be stated on the final
values and the exit is among the facts: the binary searches have it, and so do the merge and gnome sort.

\subsection{Writing a lemma that the engine applies}\label{sec:principles:matcher}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Write for the goal, not for the program.} The engine simplifies before it matches: reads are resolved,
  guards decided, cases merged. A lemma is written for the goal as the engine prints it: the first case of
  \lemma{stores\_cases\_def} stores a constant, because the read in the update was resolved before the lemma was tried
  (Section~\ref{sec:brs2}); the transpose's lemma has $\natf(n)$ as the number of inner iterations, because that is what
  the constraints determine.
\item \textbf{The left-hand side is a term of the goal.} A variable of the goal matches through its definitions
  (Section~\ref{sec:engine:classes}), so a lemma on $\cur(\mathsf{trace}(\ldots))$ applies to $b$ with $b = \cur(B)$,
  inside $\sorted(b, 0, n)$ and elsewhere. A left-hand side that the goal does not contain, e.g.\ the trace of an inner
  loop described by separate lists, never applies, however true it is.
\item \textbf{Higher-order variables abstract a term of the match.} $G(k)$ matches any term over the bound variable,
  by abstraction; its instance must contain the term it abstracts. Arithmetic on a bound variable, $a[i - 1]$, is
  matched modulo the normal form of sums, including negative literals; arithmetic on two rule variables, $m + n$,
  is not a pattern, and is written as a variable $s$ with $s = m + n$ as a side condition.
\item \textbf{Side conditions are matched while open, looked up when closed.} A condition with an open variable binds it
  from a fact; a closed one is first looked up among the facts modulo the classes and canonical forms, and only then
  decided. An iteration constraint can therefore be a closed condition, written as the goal states it, unless the
  goal's constraint has an unused or repeated dependency: the instance is simplified before the lookup, which drops
  such dependencies, and is then not found; such a condition is matched with open lists, identified with the lists of
  the left-hand side by equations (Section~\ref{sec:sorts:selection}). A quantified hypothesis is never matched; the
  specification provides an atom for it.
\item \textbf{Guards in the context of the update.} A pattern's guard matches a program's guard if its conjuncts follow
  from it in the context of the update, where the iteration constraint holds. A lemma may therefore guard its cases by
  what the loop guarantees ($lo < hi$), and may add a case for the complement, which the program's cases never need to
  cover (Section~\ref{sec:engine:cases}).
\item \textbf{Traces as rule variables.} A lemma about a trace of the goal names it by a rule variable, bound by the
  left-hand side to the goal's variable, with the equation of the trace as an open condition; the components are read
  by open variables, $x = \cur(Sv).0$, matched against the facts. A condition that contains a trace as a term has its
  views recomputed at every evaluation, and a closed read of a component is replaced by its view before it is looked up
  (Section~\ref{sec:sorts:cycles}).
\item \textbf{Dependencies.} A pattern may iterate over more dependencies than the trace, e.g.\ the counter and a loop
  variable equal to it; repeated dependencies of the pattern take the same dependency of the goal. Further
  dependencies that the update does not read, $hs$, are selected by no variable of the pattern and left aside.
\item \textbf{Variables named apart.} The variables of a lemma are not renamed apart from those of the goal: a method
  variable with the name of a rule variable can make an open condition coincide with a fact literally, so that it holds
  without binding the variable. Methods and lemmas of a program name their variables apart from those of the database
  ($x$, $n$, $Ks$, \ldots).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Soundness}\label{sec:principles:sound}
The lemmas of the database are proved in Lean; the lemmas of a program are axioms, and the abstract predicates
($\sorted$ and $\perm$ are defined in the database, but $\argmin$, $\mathsf{insert}$, $\mathsf{bubbled}$ and the other
predicates of the sorts are not) are axiomatized by them. The discipline below replaces the missing check.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \textbf{A fact must hold for every value of its terms.} A forward fact about a named trace is assumed wherever
  the term occurs, not only where a loop produced it. The bounds of the bisection hold for the loops of the programs
  but would be false for an unspecified step after the exit; the definition is made total by a case that keeps the
  state, so that the fact holds for every run (Section~\ref{sec:bisect}). The same applies to any lemma with arbitrary
  guards: a last case for their complement, with a result that keeps the conclusion true, makes complete guards
  unnecessary as a premise.
\item \textbf{An induction constrains every step.} A quantified step condition over $0 \le k < n$ with $n \le \intf(m)$
  steps leaves the further steps unconstrained. The number of steps is fixed exactly, or the invariant holds for the
  steps beyond the last meaningful one, or the result holds for any number of iterations beyond a bound.
\item \textbf{A condition must not be provable by the conclusion.} A side condition is matched in the context of the
  facts, where other rules fire; a rule that rewrites the condition using the atom that the lemma is about derives the
  atom from itself. The safe form of a condition is an atom matched literally, or a fact of the loop.
\item \textbf{Every result is checked on a wrong postcondition.} A lemma set that also closes $\sorted(b, 0, n + 1)$, the
  sum plus one, or a negated flag is unsound or vacuous. For the C programs, a false assertion leaves a residual that
  shows what the lemmas computed, e.g.\ the sum $N - 1$ against a bound $N - 2$.
\item \textbf{A killed run prints no goal.} A run that exceeds its time limit prints nothing and looks like a proof;
  every result is read with its exit status, and long runs use a private copy of the tool, since a rebuild during a run
  crashes it.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Termination}\label{sec:principles:termination}
Rewriting cycles by one mechanism. An iteration constraint as a side condition with open variables is matched against
the iteration constraints of the goal, which builds the context of each fact's combinator and rewrites the reads of
its dependencies there; if the left-hand side of the rule matches one of those rewritten reads, the rule recurs through
the same fact one level deeper, until the step budget is exhausted. Both ingredients are needed: a condition over lists
that the left-hand side binds, and a left-hand side general enough to match inside that context, such as a bare read,
a comparison of a bound variable, or an equation. A rule with such conditions therefore has a left-hand side that no
rewriting of a read can produce: an iteration constraint with a specific body, or an atom of the specification. The
order of the modules also decides which abstraction a fact gets, since rules are tried in the order of the files.
Where the budget is exceeded, the reported rule is the last step of the cycle, not its cause.

\subsection{Diagnostics}\label{sec:principles:diag}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item With \texttt{-rules}, a proof reports the lemmas it applied, per goal, including the instances of forward facts;
  a residual goal points to the missing lemma.
\item \texttt{-debug match} reports the rules on traces whose left-hand side fails, with the case of the program that no
  case of the pattern covers and the guard that fails; \texttt{-debug conditions} the closed side conditions that fail,
  with the facts on their variables; \texttt{-debug forward} the instances of forward facts; \texttt{-debug rewrite}
  every step, including the views of cycles.
\item \emph{Probes}: a copy of a lemma with its first $k$ conditions and a fresh atom as right-hand side shows in the goal
  the largest $k$ whose conditions hold. \emph{Pair runs}: the rules of a file in pairs on a program that does not
  terminate find the pair that cycles.
\item \emph{Control runs}: a lemma that is believed to fire is removed or broken, and the goal must stay open; a
  definition that is believed to fold is made a plain declaration, and the fold must stop.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The same principles on C programs}\label{sec:principles:monocera}
The MonoCera tests show the principles without hand-written specifications.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \textbf{The shape of the data decides the lemma, not the idea.} A sum kept in a cell of an array is a trace of
  stores; the lemma for it has the right-hand side of the scalar sum, so the database continues from there.
\item \textbf{Left-hand sides bind, side conditions must be facts.} \lemma{sum\_cases\_below} has the bound in its
  left-hand side, a comparison, so the bound need not be among the facts.
\item \textbf{Specific lemmas are summaries.} The strided stores and the stores ahead fix the number of stores and of
  cases on their left-hand sides, as the summary of an algorithm fixes its loop nest; strides with per-cell cases want a
  schema with the number of cases as a parameter.
\item \textbf{The checking loop is an instantiation at a witness.} The assertion of the checking loop becomes an
  iteration constraint over its earlier iterations and a goal at the current index; the step from the constraint of one
  loop to a position of another is the one of the linear search, an atom and its instantiation, and needs the closed form
  of the filled array.
\item \textbf{Lookup against inference.} Horn clauses for z3 prove none of the 125 tests: the quantified invariants
  over arrays, $b[k] = 2k^2 + 2k + 1$ for all $k < i$, are not found. A lemma found once serves every loop of its shape,
  and the price is that a loop without a lemma stays open.
\end{itemize}
