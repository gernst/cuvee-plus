\section{Migrating Lemmas to the Closed Form, and What Stays Open}\label{sec:open}

Twenty-seven of the thirty programs of the folder of algorithms are proved. Seven of them had lemmas written for the
earlier representation of one trace per variable, where an inner loop was a family of fresh lists distributed over the
outer iteration constraint; these lemmas did not match the closed form of Section~\ref{sec:teq}, and each was
rewritten for it. This section describes what each needed, as patterns for the next migration, and then the three
programs that stay open. Every migrated proof was checked on a wrong postcondition, which leaves a goal.

\subsection{Insertion sort: a pass of data-dependent length}\label{sec:open:insertion}
Insertion sort moves the new element down while it is less than its predecessor,
\texttt{while 0 < j \&\& b[j] < b[j - 1] \{ swap; j := j - 1; \}}. Unlike the passes of bubble sort, the number of
inner iterations is not determined by the bounds: the outer iteration constraint records, for every outer iteration,
that each executed swap had $b[j] < b[j - 1]$, and the exit of the pass, $j = 0$ or $b[j] \ge b[j - 1]$. The proof has
three parts.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \emph{The pass is folded.} A function $\mathsf{insert\_pass}(b, i, p, nn)$ marked \texttt{\{:fold\}} has as its
  body the inner trace as the goal states it, the swaps of the positions $j$, a counter down from $i$, and $j - 1$. In
  the variant with one pointer, \texttt{-teq} writes $j - 1$ as $k - k'$ over the outer counter $k$ and the inner one
  $k'$ (since $j = i - k'$ and $i = k + 1$), and the fold matches $-k' + p$ with $p$ a parameter; in the variant with
  two pointers it is the second counter. The fold also applies inside the constraints, so that they mention the pass
  by its name.
\item \emph{An induction over the outer trace} takes the two recorded constraints as side conditions, with
  higher-order predicates $C$ and $D$ matched against them, and decides its quantified step condition
  $\forall b, k, nn, i.\ \ldots \wedge \sorted(b, 0, k + 1) \wedge C(\ldots) \wedge D(\ldots) \Rightarrow
  \sorted(F(b, k, nn, i), 0, k + 2)$ by a step lemma on the pass.
\item \emph{The step is stated over $k$ alone}: the prefix $[0, k + 1)$ before the pass and $[0, k + 2)$ after it. The
  engine substitutes $i = k + 1$ in the bound of $\sorted$ but not in the arguments of the pass, so a step lemma
  stated over $i$ is not matched.
\end{itemize}
The permutation follows from a rule that the pass is a permutation and an induction over the steps of the outer trace.

\subsection{The transpose: a choice fixed by the constraints}\label{sec:open:transpose}
The inner loop of the transpose folds into $\mathsf{column}(x, A, k, nn)$, over the numbers $\seq{N}$ of inner
iterations, a choice, which the constraints of the outer loop fix as $\seq{N} = \bl{\consts(m', \natf(n))}$. One lemma
reads the transposed element of the outer trace of columns; it takes that equation as its side condition, which binds
the number of rows. The four lemmas of the former form, for the fragments of the inner loop and the outer trace as their
results, are no longer needed.

\subsection{The first duplicate: an early exit by a flag}\label{sec:open:flags}
The first duplicate scans the pairs $i < j$ and stops both loops when a flag is set. The inner loop folds into
$\mathsf{scan}(x, a, i, nn)$; \texttt{-split} separates the cases of the postcondition, and two rules on the atom
$\mathsf{distincts}(\ldots)$ take the final flag as it stands among the facts. If the flag is set, some scan found
$a[i] = a[j]$ with $j < n$, by the constraint that the inner loop ran only while $j < n$. If it is reset, it was reset
at the head of every outer iteration, so every scan ran up to $n$ by its exit without a match. Two details of the
engine shaped the rules. The outer counter is an open variable $K_v$, identified with $K$ by its equation: the instance
of a side condition over two equal dependencies $\bl{\cnt(0, 1, m)}$ normalizes to one dependency, whereas the facts
keep $\bl{K}$ and $\bl{\cnt(0, 1, m)}$ apart, so the instance is not found. And the outer loop's end is stated with
$m = \natf(i)$, since after \texttt{-split} the number of iterations and the final index are related by this equation
only (Section~\ref{sec:open:arith}).

\subsection{The partitions: regions of a whole cycle}\label{sec:open:partition}
In the partition around a pivot, the array and the boundary $i$ read each other, and the scan $j$ joins the cycle
through the guard; \texttt{-teq} makes them one trace of $(b, i, j)$, of $(b, eq, j, lt)$ for the three-way
partition. The counts are summarized through the views of their components, as before. The array component is not:
a summary of it with the positions $j$ stated as a counter, $\bl{\cnt(0, 1, n)}$, is never matched, since the matcher
pairs the component's dependency $\bl{S}.2$ only with a generic list. The regions of the result are therefore rules
on the atoms of the postcondition over the whole trace, e.g.
\begin{align*}
  &0 \le k < \mathsf{below}(a, p, n, \bl{\cnt(0, 1, n)}) \Rightarrow\\
  &\quad (\cur(\mathsf{trace}(a, 0, 0,\ \mathsf{for}\ \bl{\cnt(0, 1, n)}\ \mathsf{do}\ \ldots)).0[k] < p) \Leftrightarrow \mathsf{true},
\end{align*}
with the pattern a copy of the trace as the goal states it. The bound $i \le j$ needs $\mathsf{below}(\ldots) \le j$
for a number of iterations $n' = \natf(j)$, again a rule with that equation as its side condition. The views of the cycle of three
accumulators, which had looked too expensive, are computed without difficulty.

\subsection{The reversal: the exit as it stands}\label{sec:open:reverse}
The swaps of $k$ and $n - 1 - k$ are recognized as a reversal by a lemma whose bounds, $2n' \le c + 1$ and $c \le 2n'$
for the start $c = n - 1$ of the counter down, follow from the exit. The exit leaves a disjunction among the facts,
$n' = 0 \vee n' < j + 2$ for the final value $j$ of the other pointer, which the arithmetic does not split. The
summary takes that disjunction itself as its side condition, binding $j$ by matching, together with $n' + j = c$
and $j \le n'$; it is true in both cases, since with no iteration the range has at most one element. No
\texttt{-split} is needed, which would have related $n'$ and the index $i$ only by $n' = \natf(i)$.

\subsection{Engine findings}\label{sec:open:arith}
The migration needed no change of the engine, but it ran into three of its limits, each worked around in the lemmas:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \emph{$\natf$ is a one-way bridge.} An equation $n = \natf(x)$ with $0 \le x$ among the facts does not give
  $\intf(n) = x$ to the arithmetic, so a bound on $\intf(n)$ does not transfer to $x$. The lemmas take the equation
  and $0 \le x$ as side conditions and state the bound on $x$ directly.
\item \emph{Instances are normalized, facts are not.} A side condition is simplified after its instantiation, which
  merges equal dependencies and drops unused ones; the fact it should be found as keeps them. Binding the dependency
  by an open variable identified by its equation keeps the instance in the facts' form.
\item \emph{Component dependencies match only generic lists.} The history $\bl{S}.j$ of a component, as a dependency
  of the view of another component, is not matched by a counter pattern even where its normal form is a counter.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{What stays open}\label{sec:open:other}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \emph{An existential postcondition} (\texttt{max\_element}): the maximum is
  \[ m = \cur(\mathsf{maxs}(a[0], \selects(a, \bl{\cnt(1, 1, n)}))), \]
  and $\exists k.\ a[k] = m$ needs a witness. A
  lemma whose left-hand side is the existential does not apply, since the matcher has no case for quantifiers. The
  same program with the postcondition $\occurs(m, \ldots)$ in the vocabulary of the database closes; quantified
  left-hand sides, or witnesses taken from the terms of the goal, would close the other.
\item \emph{The step budget}: the cocktail sort, with two inner loops per outer iteration, and the counting sort, with
  four loops in sequence and a scatter through a table of positions, stop because the rewriting exceeds its budget,
  with a rule on counters or sums as the last step. The last step of a rewriting that does not terminate is rarely its
  cause; finding the cycle needs the rules of the file in pairs on the program, or a report of the rule applications
  that repeat. Their lemmas are those of the former form, not yet migrated.
\end{itemize}
