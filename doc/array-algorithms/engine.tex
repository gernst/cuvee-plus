\section{The Engine}\label{sec:engine}

The goals of Section~\ref{sec:teq} are proved by rewriting with a database of lemmas: an equation
$l = r$ or an equivalence $l \Leftrightarrow r$ is applied from left to right wherever its left-hand side
matches, provided its side conditions hold. A goal is proved when it rewrites to $\mathsf{true}$. Nothing is
inferred: the lemmas state what loops of a certain shape compute, and the engine's job is to find the loops
of the goal among those shapes. That job is harder than it looks, and the engine is designed around what
makes it hard. Each design decision below answers a difficulty that the examples of this document met; the
insight is stated first, then the mechanism.

\subsection{The facts form one structure, not a list}\label{sec:engine:classes}
\emph{Insight: a lemma must see a term in whatever form the goal states it.} The goal names traces by
variables, $K = \cnt(0, 1, n)$, $I = \cnt(0, 1, n)$, $b = \cur(B)$, and a lemma about counters must match $\bl{K}$
as well as $\bl{\cnt(0, 1, n)}$, and $\sorted(b, 0, n)$ as well as $\sorted(\cur(B), 0, n)$.

The facts of the context are kept in a congruence closure: a union-find over their terms, where equal
arguments make applications equal. The \emph{definitions} of a variable are the members of its class that are
not variables, in both orientations of every equation and transitively, so $K = I$ follows from two equations
with the same counter, and $x = f(y)$ with $y = z$ gives $f(z)$. When a pattern does not match a variable, the
matcher tries its definitions. Only variables have definitions: a term matched through a member of its class
that contains the term would be rewritten forever.

\subsection{Closed conditions are looked up, not proved again}\label{sec:engine:known}
\emph{Insight: a side condition whose variables are all bound is a question about the facts, not a new goal.}
A lemma's side conditions are matched against the facts while they have open variables, which binds them. Once
a condition is closed, it is first looked up: it holds if it is congruent to a fact modulo the classes, and a
condition with binders, such as an iteration constraint $\al{\ldots}{\ldots}$, holds if its canonical form
equals that of a fact. The canonical form renames bound variables by position and sorts dependencies, cases,
conjuncts and the sides of equations, so that alpha-variants and reorderings coincide. Only a condition that
is not found is decided by simplification. Without the lookup, a summary lemma that needs the iteration
constraint of an inner loop as a condition must restate it with fresh variables and identify them with the
ones of the goal by equations; with it, the condition is written as it is meant. The instance is simplified before
it is looked up, so a fact whose dependencies include one that its body does not use is not found by a condition
without it (Section~\ref{sec:sorts:selection}).

\subsection{Arithmetic is decided, not rewritten}\label{sec:engine:arith}
\emph{Insight: the bounds that make array reads meaningful are linear facts, and a decision procedure should
supply them.} The context decides linear arithmetic over the integers by a simplex with bounds; strict
comparisons are offset by one, and an atom $t / k$ for a positive constant $k$ brings its bounds
$k \cdot (t / k) \le t \le k \cdot (t / k) + k - 1$. So the midpoint of a binary search lies in its interval,
$lo \le (hi + lo) / 2 < hi$ for $lo < hi$, without a lemma. Solutions of linear equations are substituted into
polynomials.

\subsection{Views: per-variable lemmas apply to cycles}\label{sec:engine:views}
\emph{Insight: the database is written for single accumulators, but loops have cycles; the engine should
translate, not the database.} Most lemmas describe a trace of one accumulator over a counter, a sum, a store,
a bound. A variable of a cycle is a component of a trace of several accumulators, $\cur(S).0$. The engine
treats such a read as a marker and matches it modulo its \emph{view}: the trace of that accumulator alone,
with the other accumulators bound over their histories $\bl{S}.j$ as further dependencies. A lemma about
$\cur(\tr{x_0}{\bl{K}, hs}{x, k, h \mapsto \ldots})$ then applies to $\cur(S).0$ with $hs := \bl{S}.1$. The
histories are inert as dependencies, so the projections do not unfold each other, and a component is not
projected again inside its own view. A marker is replaced by its view once the view reads no component of
the trace, e.g.\ a counter by its value. Views are memoized by the facts that mention their variables, so
that positions with the same relevant facts share them.

\subsection{Whole cycles: a lemma may name the loop}\label{sec:engine:cycles}
\emph{Insight: some loops are only meaningful as a whole, and the iteration constraint belongs inside the
update.} A pattern with several accumulators matches a trace of as many, component by component: the initial
values pairwise, the results in the context of the update. In that context the histories of the trace denote
the accumulators, so every iteration constraint over the histories holds for the accumulators: inside the
update of the binary search, $lo < hi$ is a fact. This is what makes a lemma like
\begin{align*}
  &\mathsf{trace}(l_0, h_0,\ \mathsf{for}\ ks\ \mathsf{do}\ lo, hi, k \mapsto
   \upd{G(M(lo, hi)) \wedge lo < hi}{M(lo, hi) + 1}\ \ldots)\\
  &\quad = \mathsf{bisection}(l_0, h_0, ks, M, G, H)
\end{align*}
match a program whose guards do not mention $lo < hi$: the conjunct follows from the guard of the program in
the context of its update. The same mechanism decides guards that depend on the constraint in every rewrite
inside an update, not only for whole cycles.

Higher-order variables of a pattern, the probe $M$ and the guards $G$, $H$, are bound by abstracting the
matched term over the bound variables, as in the higher-order pattern fragment, and an instance applied to
arguments is beta-reduced.

\subsection{Case lists are matched in one direction}\label{sec:engine:cases}
\emph{Insight: a lemma may be total where the program is partial, as long as the context excludes the
difference.} An update of cases is matched by requiring every case of the program to be covered by a case of
the pattern, whose result matches and whose guard follows from the program's guard. Cases of the pattern that
cover none are ignored. This is an equation wherever the program has a case, and the guards of the program are
complete in the context of its update by construction: the iteration constraint states that the loop test
held, and the cases are the branches of the body. So a lemma may add a last case for the complement of its
guards, e.g.\ $\upd{\neg(lo < hi) \vee \neg(G \vee H)}{lo}$, which keeps the state. The program has no such
case, the context excludes it, and the lemma becomes true for \emph{every} update, not only those of a loop
that stops in time. Section~\ref{sec:bisect} shows why this matters for soundness.

\subsection{Forward facts: triggers instead of rewrites on atoms}\label{sec:engine:forward}
\emph{Insight: many facts about a loop are needed where only its term occurs.} A bound of the current value of
a monotone trace, or the bounds of a binary search's result, is not a rewrite of anything: no atom of the goal
mentions it. A lemma marked \texttt{\{:fw\}}, \texttt{requires C ensures φ}, is assumed wherever a term matching
its \emph{trigger} occurs in a fact; the trigger is the term of $\varphi$ with the most variables, below its
boolean and arithmetic structure. The trigger is matched like a left-hand side, through definitions and views,
and the instance names the term as it occurs, so that it reaches the classes and the linear constraints of the
goal: $\cur(\mathsf{bisection}(\ldots)).0 \le \mathsf{cut}(G, l_0, h_0)$ is assumed as
$\cur(S).0 \le \mathsf{cut}(G, 0, n)$, and the cut is then rewritten by the database to a partition point of
the array. Triggers are retried while instances are added, since one instance may enable another; this
terminates, as the terms are fixed.

\subsection{Suspension: a rule does not prove its own conditions}\label{sec:engine:suspend}
\emph{Insight: a condition that mentions the rule's own left-hand side must not be rewritten by the rule.}
While the side conditions of a rule are evaluated, the rule is suspended, and while the view of a component
is computed, that component is not projected. Without the suspension, a lemma about $\cur(L)$ with the exit
$\cur(H) \le \cur(L)$ as a condition rewrites the condition by itself and never terminates; with it, the
condition may mention the left-hand side.

\subsection{What remains a goal}\label{sec:engine:remains}
A goal that remains is split by cases on its first disjunctive premise, up to four deep, each case in a fresh
context. A goal without traces and other functions than those of arithmetic, over at most four variables, is
refuted if it evaluates to false at an assignment of small values; it becomes $\mathsf{false}$, and the
program is reported incorrect. All other goals are printed as they remain, with the lemmas that were applied,
so that a residual goal points to the missing lemma.

\subsection{The lemma database}\label{sec:engine:db}
The database is a set of Dafny files: an entry point, injected with every system, with the declarations
of the combinators, counters, the functions on lists and the lemmas on traces; and optional modules for
aggregations (sums, counts, minima and maxima), arithmetic, arrays (stores and their reads), counters,
searching (no occurrence in a prefix, the bisection of an interval), sorted arrays (sortedness, permutations,
partition points), strided stores and counters modulo a bound. A lemma is a rewrite rule (\texttt{\{:rw\}}),
a forward fact (\texttt{\{:fw\}}), or the definition of a function that is folded where its body occurs
(\texttt{\{:fold\}}). The lemmas of the entry point and of the modules are proved in Lean 4 against
statements generated from the files, with the trace combinators defined by recursion and guarded cases as
conditionals that end in $\mathsf{unspecified}$. Where a lemma has arbitrary guards, it ends in a case for
their complement (Section~\ref{sec:engine:cases}), so that complete guards are not a premise. What the
proofs still assume is listed with each lemma: reads of the lists of a loop at its iterations, prefixes not
longer than the list, lists of equal length. The lemmas written for a single program, at the beginning of its
file, are not checked; Section~\ref{sec:principles:sound} discusses how to keep them sound.
