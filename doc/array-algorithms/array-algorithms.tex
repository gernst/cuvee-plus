\begin{abstract}
A loop can be verified without an invariant if its effect is a known shape: a counter, a sum, a sequence of stores, a
bisection of an interval. This document describes the verifier Cuvée, which works this way. Symbolic execution
describes each loop exactly by a trace equation system, equations over the sequences of values of its variables, one
trace per strongly connected component of the variables that read each other; a database of lemmas, proved in Lean,
states what loops of known shapes compute; and a deduction engine rewrites the goal with the lemmas and decides the
remaining arithmetic. We describe the representation, the engine and the database with the insights that shaped them,
follow one proof step by step, and work through examples from a linear search to binary search, nested loops,
sorting and partitioning, and the C programs of the MonoCera benchmarks, showing which lemmas each proof needs and
which capabilities of the engine it relies on. Of thirty array algorithms, twenty-seven are proved. The evaluation
on SV-COMP shows the strength of the approach, arrays of unbounded size, and its weakness, numeric loops and
refutations.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Proving a loop correct usually means finding an invariant. Many loops in practice need none: they count, sum, copy,
fill, or search, and what they compute after $n$ iterations has a closed form that a lemma can state once. Verification
by lookup exploits this. Symbolic execution describes each loop by equations over the sequences of values its variables
take, the \emph{traces}; a database of lemmas rewrites traces of known shapes into their closed forms; and the
postcondition, once the traces are gone, is a formula that the engine decides. Nothing is inferred. A loop whose shape
has a lemma is proved; a loop without one stays open, and the goal that remains says which lemma is
missing.

\subsection{The tool}
\cuvee{} reads C (the dialect of SV-COMP), Dafny, Boogie, SMT-LIB and SV-LIB into one core language, on which
transformations work: verification conditions by weakest preconditions (\texttt{-vcgen}), trace equation systems
(\texttt{-teq}), and Horn clauses (\texttt{-chc}); the simplifier (\texttt{-simplify}) and the deduction engine of this
document (\texttt{-prove}); and printers back into each language or solvers for what remains. \texttt{-teq} starts at the
entry point of the program, \texttt{main} in C, and inlines the calls. A run of \cuvee{} on an SV-COMP task chains them,
e.g.\ \texttt{-teq} with the modules of the database, \texttt{-prove -split -prove}, and \texttt{-verify} for the answer.
\cuvee{} does not need to know whether a goal is provable before trying; what the engine cannot close is lowered for an
SMT solver or reported as unknown (Section~\ref{sec:engine:beyond}).

\subsection{What must work together}
Three things must work together for this, and the examples of this document test each of them:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \textbf{the representation} must expose the shapes: a counter must look like a counter, also when its variable
  is read by others, and an inner loop must be a term to which the lemmas of single loops apply
  (Section~\ref{sec:teq});
\item \textbf{the engine} must find the shapes in the goal although the goal names traces by variables, splits facts
  over many hypotheses, and states arithmetic in its own normal form (Section~\ref{sec:engine});
\item \textbf{the database} must contain the shapes, and its lemmas must be true for every term they match, not only
  for the loops they were written for (Sections~\ref{sec:engine:db} and~\ref{sec:bisect}).
\end{itemize}
The worked examples come in order of what they need. The array initialization (Section~\ref{sec:init}) is followed
step by step, from the verification condition to the closed goal, and needs the database alone. The linear search (Section~\ref{sec:lsearch}) needs an iteration
constraint named and instantiated at a position. The swap of two arrays (Section~\ref{sec:swap}) is a cycle of two
variables, which the lemmas of single loops resolve through the views of its components. Nested loops (Section~\ref{sec:nested}) need nothing beyond the
lemmas of single loops, because an inner loop is a value of the outer update; the transpose needs one lemma for the
nest. Binary search (Section~\ref{sec:bisect}) needs a lemma over the whole loop, whose two variables are meaningless
apart, and shows why such a lemma must be total to be sound. The C programs of the MonoCera benchmarks
(Section~\ref{sec:monocera}) need the database alone. Selection sort, bubble sort, gnome sort, the merge and the
distinctness check (Section~\ref{sec:sorts}) need a summary of what their loops compute and an induction or an
invariant of the whole cycle, stated for the closed form. Seven more programs had lemmas written for an earlier
representation and were migrated to the closed form; their migration, the limits of the engine it ran into, and the
three programs that stay open are discussed in Section~\ref{sec:open}. Section~\ref{sec:evaluation} reports the results on
SV-COMP and two benchmarks from the literature. Section~\ref{sec:principles} collects the principles of
writing lemmas for lookup, and Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} the insights into the representation, the engine and
soundness.

\paragraph{Notation.} Programs and lemmas are in the Dafny dialect of Cuvée, where arrays are total maps
$\mathsf{array}\langle\mathsf{int},\mathsf{int}\rangle$ and $a[i := v]$ is a store. We write $\bl{X}$ for the history of a
trace $X$, the list of its values before the last, $\cur(X)$ for its last value, and $\cnt(i, c, n)$ for the counter
$\langle i, i + c, \ldots, i + nc\rangle$ of $n$ steps. $\swap{x}{p}{q}$ abbreviates the double store
$x[p := x[q]][q := x[p]]$. Capital letters $K, I, S$ denote traces, and hatted letters $\seq{N}$ choices, lists of an outer
loop that nothing defines, such as the numbers of iterations of an inner loop. Each example is run by the command line
in the first line of its file, \texttt{-teq} to generate the trace equation system and \texttt{-prove} to prove it, with
the modules of the database that the file names.
